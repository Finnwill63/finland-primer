A country of 5.6 million produced Linux, Nokia, Supercell, Wolt, Oura and ICEYE. It spends 3.2% of GDP on R&D against an EU average of 2.2%, has legislated its way toward 4% by 2030, and holds linked genomic and health-registry data on nearly a tenth of its population. A steady stream of people come here to work out how — as students, as founders, as delegations. That visitor is who this is written for.

Most of what Americans get wrong about Finland is not the big stuff. You will not be surprised that it is cold in February or that people speak English. You will be surprised that a Finn's silence in a meeting is agreement rather than hostility, that the best meal of the day costs €13 and is served at noon, that nobody tips, that the whole country empties out in July, and that the fastest way to build a real relationship here involves taking your clothes off.

This guide covers the things that actually trip people up. It comes in two versions, because a twenty-year-old here for a semester and a research dean here for three days of meetings need almost none of the same advice.

True whichever track you take
Currency
Euro, both countries
Estonia dropped the kroon in 2011. No exchange needed between them.
Paying
Tap your card
Among the most cashless places on earth. Carry €40 as a fallback you probably won't spend.
Tipping
Genuinely not expected
Service is in the price. American-style tipping reads as odd, not generous.
English
Excellent, everywhere
You will never be stuck. Learning kiitos still lands well.
Tap water
Among the world's best
Free in every restaurant. Bringing a bottle is the easiest money you'll save.
Emergencies
112
Works from any phone, no SIM required, English spoken. The 112 Suomi app sends your GPS.
Passport
Carry it
Required for the Tallinn ferry and for bar entry. A US driver's licence is often refused.
Daylight
5h50m to 19h
The single biggest variable in a Helsinki trip. Check the month before you book anything.
One thing to check before you fly
🛂
The EU border rules changed, and are still changing. The Entry/Exit System (EES) has been fully operational since April 2026 — on your first Schengen entry you are photographed and fingerprinted at a booth instead of getting a passport stamp. No application, no fee, but build in extra time at Helsinki-Vantaa on arrival. Separately, ETIAS — a €20 pre-travel authorisation for Americans — is expected to launch in late 2026 with a grace period after. The application portal is not open yet, so any site currently taking ETIAS applications is a scam. Check travel-europe.europa.eu/etias_en, the only official source, six to eight weeks before you travel.
